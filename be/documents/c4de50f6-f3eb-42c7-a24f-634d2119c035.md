[Ninh Binh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ninh_Binh)and

## Understand

[[edit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/w/index.php?title=Northern_Vietnam&action=edit§ion=3)]

The lowland and delta areas were traditionally farming areas. Since ancient times, the region alternated between local control and domination by China. The French took control in the mid-1800s, and developed Hanoi into the capital of French Indochina. Hanoi became the center of communist resistance to the colonists, and was made the capital of an independent Vietnam as the French were pushed out. The provinces around Hanoi (Bac Ninh, Thai Nguyen, Vinh Phuc, Hung Yen, and Hai Duong) are now largely industrialized.

Toward the South China Sea is [Ha Long Bay](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ha_Long_Bay) and numerous surrounding islands, with hundreds of limestone karsts and caves. The mountainous provinces bordering China have dramatic landscapes, such as pristine subtropical forests, rice terraces and tea plantations. These provinces are less developed, with fewer industries and more ethnic groups.

Karst landscapes are scattered throughout the region. In the far north, the [Ha Giang Loop](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ha_Giang_Loop) is a popular motorcycle route, starting in [Ha Giang](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ha_Giang) and winding through the [Dong Van Plateau](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Dong_Van_Karst_Plateau_Geopark). ## Do

[[edit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/w/index.php?title=Northern_Vietnam&action=edit§ion=8)]

 Cycling is a niche but growing activity in Northern Vietnam, progressively getting more challenging the farther you venture out of Hanoi. During your ride, you will be distracted by dense jungle, misty hills and mountains and gorgeous rice terraces. But to explore the region to its fullest, you ought to be an experienced peddler. If you are skilled and brave enough, ride along the jaw-dropping Mã Pí Lèng Pass between the towns of

[Dong Van](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Dong_Van)and

[Meo Vac](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Meo_Vac?action=edit&redlink=1).

The northern mountains offer trekking opportunities, most notably around [Sa Pa](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sa_Pa) and [Ba Be National Park](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ba_Be_National_Park).

At the end of a busy day, try an herbal bath (tắm lá thuốc), an ancient tradition of some ethnic minorities, particularly the Red Dao people. Many massage parlours in towns across the region have these baths. Some appear to be appropriating this cultural practice, but some are very traditional, with families or groups having their own recipes of dozens (or up to hundreds) of herbal ingredients than are dried, then boiled until the water is fragrant, and a deep red or green colour. A 30 minute dip in one of these baths will have you feeling very relaxed.

## Buy

[[edit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/w/index.php?title=Northern_Vietnam&action=edit§ion=9)]

Most local markets in the north of Vietnam take place on a particular day each week. For example, [Dong Van](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Dong_Van) Market and [Can Cau](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Can_Cau?action=edit&redlink=1) Markets take place on Saturday or Sunday. The markets open at 05:00 and close at 15:00-16:00. Ethnic peoples, such as the Hmong, Dzao, Tay, Lo Lo and Tu Di, go to the market from the early morning on by foot, horse and motorbike. They travel in families and groups of young girls and boys in their most colorful clothes. Even if they don't sell all of their products, they still meet their old friends and have a chance to drink, chat or share their life stories. Some other markets are exceptional: They are “backward markets”! They take place every 6 days, calculated by the Lunar Calendar.
