Haiphong is mostly significant as the largest city at the delta of the Red River, which is northern Vietnam's main river and the first major river delta from the Chinese border, historically a source of military conflict and also the center of the largest major flat (i.e. agriculturally suitable) region in the country, and therefore a population center of political importance. Its use as a port region is ancient, including opium trading from upriver warlords in Yunnan (China) and general shipping from the French colonial period up to the present.

## Stay safe

[[edit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/w/index.php?title=Haiphong&action=edit§ion=11)]

Haiphong is known in Vietnam for gang activity. Maybe less well known but also historically central to transnational organized crime, particularly the French networks through most of the 20th century that supplied the American mafia.

At the turn of the millennium there were numbers published, a known resident addict population of 10,000. Later studies on injectable drug use behavior give a figure of 4,000. Haiphong municipal authorities have promoted the project of a drug-free Haiphong with multi-year targets, but the gangs are unmanaged and deterrents are weak with many old and corrupt officials still present in critical positions.

Taxis in some districts are unreliable at best. Crime rates may be lower overall in Haiphong but the attackers persistent and confident.

## Eat

[[edit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/w/index.php?title=Haiphong&action=edit§ion=13)]

Stalls about town offer the normal array of Vietnamese cuisine. Local specials include anything cua (crab) or seafood, for example:
- "bánh đa cua"- noodle soup with crab and other traditional Vietnamese ingredients. Delicious. Try it once and you won't forget it!
- "bánh bèo"- a white cake made from rice, filled with meat and eaten with a special sauce
- "bún tôm"- a kind of noodle soup, with shrimp
- "chè bưởi"- sweetened porridge, made of beans, glutinous rice and mostly grapefruit, often with added coconut sauce and dry-salted coconut slices
- "bánh mì que" or "bánh mì cay" — ultra-thin mini-baguettes stuffed with pork pâté, then dipped in a chilli, tomato and garlic sauce.

There are also quite a lot of lẩu and nướng (hotpot and BBQ respectively) restaurants.

Foreign food includes:
- A couple of alleged 'Texas BBQ' establishments
- A terrible Indian establishment
- A pricy-looking Thai restaurant
- Pizza Hut
- The Aeon Mall which has all the usual international fast food franchises, as well as the grocery store annex with a lot of typical konbini bentos and local fried monster chickens.

## Go next

[[edit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/w/index.php?title=Haiphong&action=edit§ion=16)]

[Cat Ba Island](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Cat_Ba_Island)Do Son(Beach) — 20-23 km from Haiphong. Do Son is a resort frequented by the Vietnamese, but by few Westerners. The beaches are not the greatest as silt affects the quality of the water. A visit to nearby Cat Ba is advisable if you want to swim, as the water quality is much better. Do Son sits along the ocean coast, and is filled with low-end hotels, restaurants, and karaoke bars. It has a reputation as a centre for prostitution. There is a casino (one of very few in Vietnam) at the Do Son Resort Hotel. It's worth visiting for a peaceful day or two away from the bustle of Haiphong.[Ha Long Bay](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ha_Long_Bay)[Hanoi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hanoi)— about 100 km from Haiphong, 2-3 hours.
